How it works

Five numbers, about a minute

  1. Read the question

    Five questions, the same for everyone today. Each one asks for a number: a height, a distance, a year.

  2. Give a 90% range

    Type a low and a high number so that you are 90% sure the answer is in between.

  3. See how you did

    The answer and its source come right after each question. At the end: your grid, today’s average and your streak.

FAQ

Questions

What does “90% sure” mean?

If you give ten ranges you are truly 90% sure of, about nine of them should contain the answer. So a well-calibrated player catches 4 or 5 of the 5 on most days. In studies, most people’s 90% ranges catch the answer far less often than that.

How is my score worked out?

A range catches the answer when the answer lies between your low and high number, ends included. Your score is how many of the five you caught. A range so wide it can’t miss tells you nothing; the aim is ranges that are right about 9 times in 10.

Do I need an account?

No. There are no accounts and no tracking. Your browser keeps a random id so your streak survives a reload; we never store your IP address, your name or an email. Details are in the privacy policy.

Where do the questions come from?

From Wikidata and official sources. Every answer shows its source, each day’s questions are checked by hand the night before, and you can report a question that looks wrong right under its answer.

When is the next game?

Every day at 00:00 UTC. Everyone plays the same five questions that day, so you can compare grids.

Is there a longer version?

Yes. The full assessment takes 5 minutes: twelve two-choice questions with a confidence rating and six ranges, then a chart of how sure you were against how often you were right.
